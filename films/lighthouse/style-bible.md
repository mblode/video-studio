# The Last Watch — style bible

## Format

- Photorealistic cinematic short
- 16:9, 720p generation, 24 fps look
- Real-time motion; no slow motion
- Mostly 35mm and 50mm-feeling compositions, with macro inserts for machinery
- Restrained film grain, natural lens flare, real spray and condensation

## World

An unnamed lighthouse on a treeless rock headland. The tower is whitewashed
stone with an iron spiral stair, a brass-and-steel clockwork drive, and a great
Fresnel lens. Nothing locates the story in a particular country. The equipment
feels maintained rather than picturesque.

The film moves through one weather system:

1. Steel-blue dusk, long grass bending before the storm
2. Warm pools of tungsten inside the tower
3. Near-black storm exteriors cut by white lightning and amber beam light
4. Pale silver dawn after rain
5. Clear indigo at the next dusk

## Character continuity

### The keeper

- Man in his late sixties
- Lean weathered face, grey stubble, deep-set eyes
- Navy wool coat over a charcoal work shirt
- Faded red wool scarf
- Heavy black boots
- Deliberate economical movement
- Right hand operates the brass winding key; left palm is injured in s08

### The relief

- Woman in her early thirties
- Cropped dark curls, alert dark eyes
- Mustard-yellow oilskin over a dark teal sweater
- Brown canvas tool roll and compact grey electrical meter
- Fast precise hands, still posture while observing
- Carries the brass winding key from s10 onward

The characters never change clothes. Wetness increases through the storm and
clears at dawn.

## Hero objects

**Brass winding key.** Keeper-only in s01–s09; offered in s10; accepted in s12;
used beside the relief’s meter in s13. It is the visible handover.

**Grey electrical meter.** Dismissed in s03; discovers the broken circuit in
s09; shares the final frame with the key in s13. It is new knowledge, not a
replacement for old knowledge.

**Two enamel mugs.** First appear separated in s02; sit together in s12. A quiet
relationship meter.

**The beam.** Dark in s01, working in s05, frozen in s06, absent at sea in s07,
restored in s10, trusted in s11, inherited in s13.

## Camera grammar

- Exterior geography: deliberate wides, strong horizon, people small against
  the tower and sea
- Human disagreement: stable medium two-shots with machinery dividing the frame
- Crisis: tighter lenses, lower angles, faster lateral moves, hard cuts
- Cooperation: hands and eyelines share the same frame for the first time
- Resolution: return to the composed wides from the opening

The camera never floats without purpose. Lightning is a cut accent, not a
constant effect.

## Sound grammar

Generated clips contain only:

- wind pressure, rain, surf, gulls
- boots, doors, cloth, breath
- radio static and an alarm bell without intelligible speech
- clockwork ticks, chain, gears, motor, glass resonance
- boat engine, hull impacts, signal horn

No per-shot music, dialogue, voiceover, or subtitles. A restrained score can be
mixed across the finished cut with `vs stitch --music`.

## Exclusions

- No readable labels, logbook text, call signs, maps, dates, flags, or uniforms
- No modern branding
- No sepia nostalgia or fantasy glow
- No immaculate museum surfaces
- No extra workers, crowds, or rescue crews
- No character likeness drift, wardrobe changes, or age changes
- No black opening frame, subtitles, watermarks, or in-model titles
